Antibiotic Awareness Week

NOVEMBER 18–24, 2026
Use them wisely, keep them working

Antibiotics save lives, but every use gives bacteria an opportunity to develop resistance. During Antibiotic Awareness Week, we join the World Health Organization and CDC in promoting the appropriate use of these medicines so they remain effective for every patient who needs them.1,2

The big picture

A present threat2.8 million+resistant infections and 35,000+ deaths each year in the U.S.3
A global problem1 in 6lab-confirmed bacterial infections worldwide were resistant in 20234
A growing burden39 milliondeaths worldwide forecast to be directly caused by resistance from 2025 to 20505
Did you know?Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928. In his 1945 Nobel Prize lecture, he warned that inadequate doses could allow bacteria to become resistant.6

What you can do

Everyone

  • Clean your hands, cover coughs, and stay home when sick.7
  • Stay up to date on vaccines, such as the flu vaccine.7
  • If you are prescribed an antibiotic, take it exactly as prescribed, and do not save or share it.7
  • Test your knowledge in the Stewardship Learning Challenge.

Prescribers and pharmacists

  • Reassess antibiotic therapy as results return, and narrow, convert to oral, or stop when appropriate.
  • Evaluate reported penicillin allergies before selecting a broader-spectrum alternative.8
  • Use the shortest effective duration. Each additional 10 days of therapy is associated with about a 3% higher risk of adverse events.9

Nurses and care teams

  • Practice hand hygiene. Resistant organisms can spread on surfaces and on the hands of healthcare workers.10
  • Collect cultures before the first antibiotic dose whenever possible.
  • Ask about the details of reported drug allergies and document them.8

Leaders and managers

  • Promote appropriate antibiotic use as a patient safety and quality priority.
  • Recognize stewardship efforts in huddles and staff meetings.
  • Support time for stewardship education within your department.
Did you know?In children, antibiotic side effects are the most common cause of medication-related emergency department visits.7

The Stewardship Learning Challenge

Raising awareness starts with understanding the evidence. The Stewardship Learning Challenge is an interactive educational tool that presents this week's key messages through 12 short, evidence-based games, covering when antibiotics are and are not indicated, selecting the narrowest effective therapy, and appropriate treatment durations. Each answer includes an explanation and its source.
